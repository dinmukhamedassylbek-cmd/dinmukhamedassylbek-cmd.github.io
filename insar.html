Problem

How can repeated SAR observations reveal small surface changes?

Interferometric SAR compares the phase of repeated radar acquisitions. After geometric alignment and removal of known phase components, the residual phase can be related to line-of-sight displacement — provided that coherence is sufficient and the phase is correctly unwrapped.

The project focuses on a reproducible processing chain and on understanding how short revisit intervals influence coherence and the reliability of deformation products.

Processing workflow

From raw SLC scenes to displacement.

01

TOPSAR Split

Select the required subswath, polarization and bursts covering the study area.

02

Apply Orbit File

Improve satellite orbit information before precise co-registration.

03

Back-Geocoding + ESD

Co-register master and slave data and refine burst alignment.

04

TOPSAR Deburst

Merge burst boundaries into a continuous image for subsequent processing.

05

Interferogram + coherence

Form interferometric phase and estimate coherence as a key quality indicator.

06

Topographic phase removal

Use a DEM to remove the phase contribution caused by topography.

07

SNAPHU unwrapping

Convert wrapped phase into a continuous phase field.

08

Phase to displacement

Transform unwrapped phase into line-of-sight displacement.

09

Terrain Correction + GeoTIFF

Geocode the result for GIS analysis and presentation.
